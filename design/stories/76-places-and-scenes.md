# Feature Story 76: Places and Scenes

**Status**: Paused (written 2026-11-06). Tyler wants to think more before committing; the near-term pieces (party token, map markers, voting) are being prototyped separately.
**Related**: Story 75 (Map Discovery), the adventure map, the Enemies Gallery (NPC library).

## The idea

Like Might and Magic's big first-person view with a small automap: the **map** says where the party is in the city (party token, landmarks, the route); the **scene** says what it's like to be there (the inn's interior, the beggar's face). The DM chooses when a scene opens. On players' screens it takes over the main space and the map shrinks to a corner; on phones the scene goes full screen with a small map button (decided).

## The pieces

1. **Places**: landmark pins the DM prepares: a name, a picture (the inn's interior), a short description, and optionally the people there. Public in a city (everyone knows the landmarks).
2. **Hidden pins**: things players don't know yet (the beggar on the way). Revealed when met; players tap for the picture and what the DM shares ("he keeps staring at Quietclaw").
3. **Party token**: "you make your way to the Green Dragon" = drag the token, or **Go here** on a place (optionally opening its scene).
4. **Scene panel**: players see the picture, name, people present (tap for a card) and description; the DM has Showing: …, Close, Switch scene.

## What would make it worthwhile

- **Cheap prep**: a place is a picture plus a name. Location art via the same Midjourney prompt workflow as item art.
- **One or two taps in play**: Go here · Show scene · Reveal. It amplifies the DM's narration, never replaces it.
- **Memory**: places visited, people met, rumours heard build a journal between sessions (ties into the Chronicle).
- **Player choice**: visible landmarks invite "let's go to the temple"; a Suggest tap lets quieter players steer.
- **Hand-off to combat**: if the beggar is bait, the scene's people become enemy tokens in one step.

## Still open (Tyler, 2026-11-06)

- **People, not just enemies**: scenes need non-enemy NPCs (townsfolk, contacts) that can also become tokens. That makes adventure much like the combat map, with the DM loading the tokens that suit adventure vs combat and switching on the fly. Valuable, but not yet sure it's the right approach.
- **Interior maps**: not now.
- Keep it tabletop-first: facilitate the DM's game rather than turn into a multiplayer RPG.
